## How Much Internet Speed Do You Need for Streaming?

Here's the honest answer that ISPs don't want you to hear: **you don't need gigabit internet to stream.** Even 4K streaming on multiple TVs works fine on a 200 Mbps connection. Here are the actual bandwidth requirements:

### Per-Device Streaming Requirements

| Quality | Speed Per Device | Service |
|---------|-----------------|---------|
| SD (480p) | 3 Mbps | Any |
| HD (720p) | 5 Mbps | YouTube, Hulu |
| Full HD (1080p) | 10 Mbps | Netflix, Disney+, HBO Max |
| 4K Ultra HD | 25 Mbps | Netflix 4K, YouTube 4K |
| 4K HDR Dolby Vision | 40 Mbps | Apple TV+, Netflix Premium |
| Live TV (YouTube TV) | 10-15 Mbps | YouTube TV, Hulu Live |

### Household Speed Recommendations

| Household | Activity | Recommended Speed |
|-----------|----------|-------------------|
| 1 person | 1 HD stream + browsing | 50 Mbps |
| 2 people | 2 HD streams + video call | 100 Mbps |
| Family of 4 | 3-4 streams + gaming + smart home | 200-300 Mbps |
| Large household | 5+ simultaneous 4K streams | 300-500 Mbps |

**The math**: Even a household running three 4K streams, a video call, and smart home devices simultaneously uses about 130 Mbps. A 200-300 Mbps plan handles this with headroom to spare.

## Why Data Caps Matter for Streamers

**Heavy streaming uses a LOT of data:**

| Quality | Data Per Hour | Monthly Use (4 hrs/day) |
|---------|-------------|------------------------|
| HD (1080p) | 3 GB | 360 GB |
| 4K | 7 GB | 840 GB |
| 4K HDR | 10 GB | 1,200 GB |

A household streaming 4K content 4 hours a day uses about **840 GB per month** — on a single TV. Add two more TVs and a few gamers, and you're looking at 1.5-2 TB monthly.

**Providers with 1 TB data caps** (like some AT&T DSL plans or certain Cox/Xfinity plans) can cause problems for streaming households. Every provider on our recommended list offers **no data cap**, which is essential for serious streamers.

## Optimize Your Home Network for Streaming

### 1. Position Your Router Centrally
Place your WiFi router in a central location, not tucked in a closet or behind furniture. WiFi signal weakens with distance and obstacles.

### 2. Use 5 GHz WiFi for Streaming Devices
Most modern routers broadcast on 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz bands. Connect streaming devices to the 5 GHz band — it's faster and less congested.

### 3. Consider Ethernet for Your Main TV
If your primary TV is far from the router, run an ethernet cable or use a powerline adapter. Wired connections eliminate WiFi variability and provide the most consistent streaming experience.

### 4. Upgrade Your Router If It's Old
If your router is 4+ years old, a new WiFi 6 router ($80-150) can dramatically improve streaming performance throughout your home. Modern routers handle multiple simultaneous streams much better than older models.

### 5. Use a Mesh WiFi System for Large Homes
For homes over 2,000 sq ft or multi-story layouts, a mesh WiFi system ensures strong signal in every room. This eliminates buffering in bedrooms and far-flung corners of the house.
