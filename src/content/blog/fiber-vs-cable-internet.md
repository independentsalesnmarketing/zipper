## Fiber vs Cable: The Quick Answer

**[Fiber internet](/internet-types/fiber) is better** for most households in 2026. It offers faster speeds, symmetrical upload/download, lower latency, and greater reliability. [Cable internet](/internet-types/cable) is still a strong choice where fiber isn't available — it's widely accessible and offers speeds up to 1 Gbps in many areas.

## Speed Comparison

| Feature | Fiber | Cable |
|---------|-------|-------|
| Max Download | Up to 10 Gbps | Up to 1.2 Gbps |
| Max Upload | Up to 10 Gbps | Up to 50 Mbps |
| Latency | 1-4ms | 10-30ms |
| Consistency | Very stable | Slows during peak hours |

### Why Upload Speed Matters

Cable's biggest weakness is upload speed. If you work from home, video conference, livestream, or upload large files, fiber's symmetrical speeds make a massive difference. Cable typically offers 10-50 Mbps upload — fiber gives you 100-1000+ Mbps.

## Reliability

Fiber internet transmits data via light pulses through glass strands. This means:

- **No electromagnetic interference** — fiber isn't affected by weather or nearby electrical equipment
- **No signal degradation** over distance — same speed whether you're near or far from the provider's node
- **No congestion** — dedicated fiber lines don't share bandwidth with neighbors like cable does

Cable uses coaxial lines shared among neighborhood nodes. During peak evening hours (7-11 PM), you may notice slowdowns as everyone streams simultaneously.

This is where cable still wins. Cable internet is available to ~90% of U.S. households. Fiber reaches about 50% and growing rapidly. Major fiber expansions are happening from AT&T, Frontier, and regional providers.
