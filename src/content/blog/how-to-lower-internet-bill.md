## The Average American Overpays for Internet

The average U.S. household pays **$75/mo** for internet service — but comparable plans from most major providers start at **$40-55/mo**. The difference? Expired promotional pricing, unnecessary equipment rentals, and plans with more speed than you actually need.

Here are 10 strategies to bring your bill down to what you should be paying.

## 1. Call and Negotiate (Potential Savings: $10-40/mo)

This is the single most effective way to lower your bill. Call your provider and use this script:

**"Hi, I've been a customer for [X months/years]. I noticed my bill has gone up to [$current price] and I'm seeing offers from [competitor] for [$competitor price]. I'd like to see if there are any promotions or loyalty discounts available to keep me as a customer."**

### Tips for Negotiating
- **Ask for the retention department** — They have authority to offer discounts that regular reps can't
- **Be polite but firm** — You're not threatening — you're simply price-shopping
- **Have a competitor offer ready** — Know exactly what T-Mobile, Frontier, or another competitor charges
- **Be willing to switch** — The best deals come when you genuinely might leave
- **Call at the end of your billing cycle** — Less urgency = reps are more willing to help

**Success rate**: About 70% of callers who ask for a discount get one. Average savings: $15-30/mo.

## 2. Drop the Equipment Rental (Savings: $5-20/mo)

Most providers charge $5-15/mo for a WiFi router and sometimes $10-15/mo for a modem. That's $120-300/year for equipment you can buy outright:

| Equipment | Provider Rental | Purchase Price | Break-Even |
|-----------|---------------|---------------|------------|
| Cable modem (DOCSIS 3.1) | $10-15/mo | $70-100 | 5-10 months |
| WiFi 6 router | $5-15/mo | $80-150 | 6-15 months |
| Modem/router combo | $15-25/mo | $150-200 | 6-13 months |

After the break-even point, you save every month. Plus, purchased equipment is often better quality than rental units.

**Note**: Fiber customers (AT&T, Verizon Fios) typically must use the provider's gateway. Cable customers have the most flexibility with their own equipment.

## 3. Downgrade Your Speed Plan (Savings: $10-40/mo)

Most people pay for more speed than they use. Run a [speed test](/speed-test) during your typical usage and check — if you're using 80 Mbps on a 500 Mbps plan, downgrade to 300 Mbps and pocket the difference.

| Speed Tier | Typical Price | Good For |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| 300 Mbps | $50/mo | 1-3 people, streaming, browsing |
| 500 Mbps | $65/mo | 3-5 people, gaming + streaming |
| 1 Gbps | $80-90/mo | 5+ people, content creation |

**When NOT to downgrade**: If multiple people work from home with concurrent video calls, or if you frequently download large files (games, 4K movies), the higher tier pays for itself in time saved.

## 4. Drop the TV Bundle (Savings: $30-80/mo)

If you have a TV+internet bundle, calculate the actual cost of the TV portion — including broadcast fees, regional sports fees, and equipment rental. In most cases, internet-only + streaming is cheaper.

| Scenario | Monthly Cost |
|----------|-------------|
| Internet + Cable TV bundle (real cost) | $160-220/mo |
| Internet only + YouTube TV | $123-140/mo |
| Internet only + streaming mix | $70-100/mo |

**How to split**: Call and ask: "I'd like to keep internet only and remove TV. What's my internet-only rate?"

## 5. Switch Providers (Savings: $10-30/mo)

New customer promotions are real money. If your rate has increased post-promo and your provider won't negotiate:

1. Sign up with a competitor at their promotional rate
2. Cancel your old provider when the new service is active
3. Return old equipment
4. Enjoy lower pricing for 12+ months

With no-contract plans from [Spectrum](/provider/spectrum), [Frontier](/provider/frontier), [Verizon](/provider/verizon), [AT&T](/provider/att), and [T-Mobile](/provider/tmobile), switching is painless.

This is free money for something you should probably be doing anyway.

## 7. Use Your Phone Plan Discount (Savings: $10-20/mo)

Some wireless carriers offer discounts if you bundle home internet with your phone plan:

| Carrier | Home Internet Discount |
|---------|----------------------|
| T-Mobile | $10/mo off with Go5G+ ($40/mo total) |
| Verizon | $15/mo off with premium phone plan ($35/mo total) |

If you're already paying for a premium phone plan, adding home internet at a discounted rate can significantly lower your total telecom bill.

## 8. Check for Low-Income Programs (Savings: $20-50/mo)

If you receive government assistance, you may qualify for dramatically reduced rates:

- **Spectrum Internet Assist**: $17.99/mo for 30 Mbps
- **AT&T Access**: $30/mo for 100 Mbps
- **Optimum Advantage Internet**: $14.99/mo for 30 Mbps
- **Federal Lifeline**: $9.25/mo discount on broadband

See our guide to [cheap internet providers](/blog/cheapest-internet-providers) for more low-cost options.

## 9. Review Your Bill for Mystery Charges

Go through your bill line by line. Common unnecessary charges:

- **WiFi security suite**: $5-10/mo (your router has built-in security)
- **Cloud backup service**: $5/mo (use free alternatives like Google Drive)
- **Static IP address**: $15-20/mo (you don't need this for home use)
- **Premium tech support**: $10-15/mo (search online for free help)

Call and remove any add-ons you don't recognize or use.

## 10. Time Your Purchase Right

Internet deals follow patterns:
- **Back-to-school (August-September)**: Providers compete for student sign-ups
- **Moving season (May-August)**: Best new-customer deals
- **Black Friday/Cyber Monday**: Waived installation fees, bonus speeds
- **End of quarter (March, June, September, December)**: Sales reps push harder to meet quotas
