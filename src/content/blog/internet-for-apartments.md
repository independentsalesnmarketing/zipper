## Internet for Apartments: What's Different?

Getting internet in an apartment comes with unique challenges:

- **Limited provider choices** — Many complexes have exclusive agreements with one cable provider
- **Shared infrastructure** — Cable internet speeds can slow during peak hours when neighbors are streaming
- **No permanent installation** — You'll need to return equipment and cancel when you move
- **Lease length uncertainty** — Contracts that extend beyond your lease are risky

Here's how to navigate these challenges and get the best internet in your apartment.

## How to Check What's Available in Your Apartment

### Step 1: Ask Your Building Manager
Before choosing a provider, ask your leasing office:
- "Which internet providers serve this building?"
- "Is there an exclusive/preferred provider agreement?"
- "Is the building wired for fiber?"

Some buildings have bulk internet deals included in rent — check if that applies before buying a separate plan.

### Step 2: Check Our Availability Tool
Use our [availability checker](/check-availability) with your apartment's exact address (including unit number). Provider availability can vary between buildings on the same street.

### Step 3: Verify Speed Claims
After signing up, run a [speed test](/speed-test) to verify you're getting advertised speeds. In apartments with shared cable infrastructure, peak-hour speeds may be lower than advertised.

## Dealing with Exclusive Provider Agreements

Many apartment complexes have agreements that give one provider exclusive access to the building's wiring. This doesn't mean you have zero alternatives:

- **Verizon 5G Home Internet and AT&T Air** don't use the building's wiring — they work through cellular networks. Even if your building has a cable exclusivity deal, you can still use fixed wireless.
- **Starlink** works in apartments with a balcony or window with southern sky exposure
- **Mobile hotspot plans** from carriers can bridge the gap for light users

If your only wired option is expensive or slow, fixed wireless is often a legitimate alternative.

## Tips for Apartment Internet

### 1. Don't Sign a Contract Longer Than Your Lease
If your lease is 12 months, don't commit to a 24-month internet contract. Most providers on our list are month-to-month, so this shouldn't be an issue.

### 2. Buy Your Own Router
Even in an apartment, buying your own router saves $5-15/mo over renting from the provider. A WiFi 6 router ($80-120) works well in apartment-sized spaces.

### 3. Use 5 GHz WiFi to Avoid Interference
Apartments are dense WiFi environments. Your neighbors' networks can interfere with yours. Use the 5 GHz band (faster, shorter range, less congested) rather than 2.4 GHz.

### 4. Consider a WiFi 6E Router
WiFi 6E routers use the uncrowded 6 GHz band, which is ideal for apartments where the 2.4 and 5 GHz bands are saturated with neighboring networks.

### 5. Test Before You Commit
Most providers offer a 14-30 day trial/return window. Test your speeds at different times of day before committing long-term. Peak evening hours (7-10 PM) are when apartment internet is most likely to slow down.
