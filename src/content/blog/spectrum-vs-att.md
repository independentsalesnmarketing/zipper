## Spectrum vs AT&T: The Quick Answer

**[AT&T Fiber](/provider/att-fiber)** wins on speed, upload performance, and reliability. **[Spectrum](/provider/spectrum)** wins on availability and simplicity. If AT&T Fiber is available at your address, choose AT&T. If only AT&T DSL is available, Spectrum cable is better. It's that straightforward.

## Full Plan Comparison

| Feature | [Spectrum](/provider/spectrum) | [AT&T Fiber](/provider/att-fiber) | AT&T DSL |
|---------|---------|-----------|---------|
| Technology | Cable | Fiber | DSL |
| Min Speed | 300 Mbps | 300 Mbps | 10 Mbps |
| Max Speed | 1 Gbps | 5 Gbps | 100 Mbps |
| Upload Speed | 10-35 Mbps | Symmetrical | 1-20 Mbps |
| Starting Price | $49.99/mo | $55/mo | $55/mo |
| Data Cap | None | None | 1 TB |
| Contract | No | No | No |
| Equipment Fee | $5/mo (router) | Free (gateway) | $10/mo |

At 500 Mbps and above, AT&T Fiber becomes the better deal — especially when you factor in symmetrical upload speeds that Spectrum can't match.

## Speed & Performance

### Download Speeds
Both providers deliver close to advertised download speeds. AT&T Fiber edges ahead with more consistent performance during peak hours, since fiber doesn't share bandwidth with neighbors like cable does.

### Upload Speeds — AT&T Wins Decisively
This is the biggest differentiator:

| Plan | Spectrum Upload | AT&T Fiber Upload |
|------|----------------|-------------------|
| 300 Mbps | 10 Mbps | **300 Mbps** |
| 500 Mbps | 20 Mbps | **500 Mbps** |
| 1 Gbps | 35 Mbps | **1,000 Mbps** |

AT&T Fiber delivers **30x more upload speed** than Spectrum at the entry level. For video calls, cloud storage, streaming to Twitch, or backing up photos — this difference is enormous.

### Latency
- **AT&T Fiber**: 5-12ms typical
- **Spectrum Cable**: 10-25ms typical

Both are fine for gaming and video calls, but fiber's lower latency gives AT&T the edge for competitive gamers.

## Reliability

**AT&T Fiber**: Fiber optic cables are resistant to weather, electromagnetic interference, and don't share bandwidth. Service is remarkably consistent.

**Spectrum Cable**: Generally reliable, but cable networks can slow during evening peak hours when the neighborhood is streaming. Occasional weather-related outages with coax infrastructure.

**Winner**: AT&T Fiber

## Customer Service

Neither provider is known for stellar customer service, but AT&T has invested heavily in improving:

| BBB Rating | B+ | A- |
| Wait Time (avg) | 12 min | 8 min |
| In-Store Locations | 700+ | 5,000+ |

AT&T's massive retail presence gives it an advantage for in-person support. Spectrum's stores are less widespread but growing.

## Availability

**Spectrum**: Available in **41 states**, primarily in urban and suburban areas. One of the most widely available cable ISPs in the country.

**AT&T Fiber**: Available in approximately **25% of U.S. households**, concentrated in major metro areas in the South, Midwest, and Southwest.

**AT&T DSL**: Broader availability than AT&T Fiber, but speeds are often 25 Mbps or less. Not recommended when cable alternatives exist.

If you're in a market where both Spectrum and AT&T Fiber are available, you're lucky — they compete aggressively on price in those overlapping areas.

## Which Should You Choose?

### Choose AT&T Fiber if:
- AT&T Fiber (not DSL) is available at your address
- You work from home and need reliable upload speeds
- You want the lowest latency for gaming
- You need speeds above 1 Gbps
- You want equipment included at no extra cost

### Choose Spectrum if:
- AT&T Fiber isn't available (but AT&T DSL is — Spectrum cable beats DSL)
- You want the simplest possible experience with one plan tier
- The $5/mo price difference at 300 Mbps matters to your budget
- You hate having any equipment restrictions (Spectrum lets you bring your own modem)

### Skip Both if:
- Verizon 5G Home Internet or Frontier Fiber is available — both offer competitive plans with no contracts
- T-Mobile 5G Home Internet is available and you just need basic service

## The Bottom Line

**AT&T Fiber is the better service** when available. Symmetrical speeds, better reliability, and competitive pricing at 500+ Mbps tiers make it the clear choice. But "when available" is the operative phrase — Spectrum's broader footprint means it's the default option in many areas.
