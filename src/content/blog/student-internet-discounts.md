Whether you're in an off-campus apartment, a house with roommates, or need a backup for unreliable dorm WiFi, having the right internet plan saves money and headaches. Here's how to get the best deal as a student.

## Student-Specific Discounts

### Provider Programs
- **Spectrum Internet Assist**: $17.99/mo for 30 Mbps — available to students receiving Pell Grants
- **AT&T Access**: $30/mo for 100 Mbps — available to Pell Grant recipients
- **Windstream Internet First**: $9.95/mo for 25 Mbps — qualifying students

### Government Programs
- **Federal Lifeline**: $9.25/mo discount for qualifying households
- Check your state's broadband assistance programs — many have student-specific provisions

### The Eligibility Loophole
Many low-income internet programs accept **Pell Grant recipients**. If you receive a Pell Grant, you qualify for programs from Spectrum, AT&T, and others — even if you don't meet other income criteria. Contact the provider's customer service and mention your Pell Grant eligibility.

## Campus WiFi vs Your Own Internet

### When Campus WiFi Is Enough
- You live in a dorm with fiber-backed WiFi
- You mostly use internet for web browsing and email
- You don't game, stream 4K, or need reliable video calls
- The campus network covers your study and living spaces

### When You Need Your Own Internet
- Campus WiFi is unreliable or congested (common at large universities)
- You live off-campus in an apartment or house
- You game online and need low latency
- You work remotely or freelance and need reliable connectivity
- You work with large files (design, video editing, development)

## Internet for Different Living Situations

### Off-Campus Apartment
**Best option**: Whichever wired provider (cable or fiber) serves your apartment complex. Check with your landlord or leasing office first — some buildings have exclusive agreements.

### Shared House
**Best option**: Highest-speed plan available, split between roommates. 300-500 Mbps handles 3-5 people comfortably.

### Living at Home
**Best option**: Ask your parents about their current plan. If they're overpaying, help them negotiate or switch (win-win: better internet for you, lower bills for them).

### Summer/Short-Term Housing
**Best option**: T-Mobile 5G Home Internet — no contract, take it with you, cancel when done. Perfect for summer sublets and temporary housing.

## Tips for Student Internet

1. **Don't sign contracts longer than your lease** — Most providers are no-contract now, but verify before signing
2. **Set up your own router** — Even in apartments with provided internet, a personal router gives you better WiFi and privacy
3. **Use ethernet for critical activities** — Plug in via ethernet for online exams, video interviews, and competitive gaming
4. **Track your usage** — Know how much bandwidth you actually use before choosing a plan
5. **Check for student verification** — Some discounts require a .edu email or enrollment verification
